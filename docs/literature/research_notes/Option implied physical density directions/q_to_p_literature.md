# Option-implied (Q) information for forecasting the physical (P) distribution of S&P 500 returns

Verification legend: [V] = existence and the quoted finding were checked by web search in this session (abstract, repository or RePEc record). [U] = a well-known paper cited from prior knowledge whose bibliographic details were NOT re-checked in this session. Treat [U] details (pages, exact numbers) as needing a check before final citation. No result is invented. Where only the abstract was seen, that is stated.

## Q1. Risk-neutral-to-physical transformations (pricing kernel / risk-aversion calibration): methods, horizons, metrics, findings

### Takeaway
The best direct evidence for S&P 500 density forecasting (Shackleton, Taylor & Yu 2010) is split by horizon. At 2-4 weeks, risk-transformed RNDs beat historical (ARCH) densities on out-of-sample log-likelihood. At 1 day, historical densities win. Mixtures of transformed-RND and historical densities beat both at short horizons. The implied risk-aversion parameters vary with horizon and volatility regime, so the calibration is horizon- and regime-specific, and the shape of the empirical pricing kernel is still disputed (the "pricing kernel puzzle").

### Cited Findings
- [V] Bliss & Panigirtzoglou (2004), "Option-Implied Risk Aversion Estimates," *Journal of Finance* 59(1), 407-446, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00637.x. Method: apply a utility function (power or exponential) to the RND from option cross-sections, and choose the risk-aversion parameter so that the transformed (P) densities are best calibrated against realised outcomes (Berkowitz-type test on PITs). Findings: relative risk aversion falls as the forecast horizon lengthens, is lower in high-volatility periods, and is consistent across utility functions and markets (S&P 500, FTSE 100) at a given horizon. — [EconBiz record](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/option-implied-risk-aversion-estimates-bliss-robert/10005303188); [Chicago Fed WP 2001-15](https://www.chicagofed.org/~/media/publications/working-papers/2001/wp2001-15r3-pdf.pdf)
- [V, secondary] A later paper describes Bliss-Panigirtzoglou Table V as showing relative risk aversion of roughly 8 at a 1-week horizon falling to roughly 2 at 6 weeks (their upper horizon). This was NOT checked against the original table. — [arXiv 1910.14522 "Option-based Equity Risk Premiums"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.14522)
- [V] Liu, Shackleton, Taylor & Xu (2007), "Closed-form transformations from risk-neutral to real-world distributions," *Journal of Banking & Finance* 31(5), 1501-1520, DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2006.09.005. Two parametric risk transformations (a utility/pricing-kernel transform and a statistical calibration transform) are applied to two RND families (generalised beta, lognormal mixture) and estimated by maximising the likelihood of observed index levels (FTSE 100 data, per the authors' related work). Main finding: a combination of parametric real-world (transformed) and historical densities gives the preferred predictive densities. — [Lancaster eprints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/44582/); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v31y2007i5p1501-1520.html)
- [V] Shackleton, Taylor & Yu (2010), "A multi-horizon comparison of density forecasts for the S&P 500 using index returns and option prices," *Journal of Banking & Finance* 34(11), 2678-2693, DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2010.05.006. Data 1991-2004. RNDs come from SV diffusion and SV jump-diffusion models fitted to options. Three transformations are used to get real-world densities. Benchmarks are ARCH-type historical densities from daily and 5-minute returns. Ranking criterion: out-of-sample likelihood of observed index levels (i.e. log score). Findings: "for horizons of two and four weeks the best forecasts are obtained from risk-transformations of the risk-neutral densities, while the historical forecasts are superior for the one-day horizon." Mixtures of real-world and historical densities have higher likelihoods than either component at short horizons. — [Lancaster eprints PDF](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/45542/1/10.pdf); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:34:y:2010:i:11:p:2678-2693)
- [V] Kostakis, Panigirtzoglou & Skiadopoulos (2011), "Market Timing with Option-Implied Distributions: A Forward-Looking Approach," *Management Science* 57(7), 1231-1249, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.1110.1346. They build one-month constant-maturity S&P 500 implied PDFs (Bliss-Panigirtzoglou 2002 smoothing), apply a risk adjustment, and form out-of-sample risky/risk-free portfolios. Investors using risk-adjusted implied distributions outperform those using historical distributions across several metrics and utility functions, net of transaction costs. Market-timing ability deteriorated during the subprime crisis. This is an economic-value test, not a log-score test. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:57:y:2011:i:7:p:1231-1249); [WP PDF (typeset)](https://typeset.io/pdf/market-timing-with-option-implied-distributions-a-forward-13mnw5z3xl.pdf)
- [V] Høg & Tsiaras (2011), "Density forecasts of crude-oil prices using option-implied and ARCH-type models," *Journal of Futures Markets* 31(8), 727-754. Crude-oil RNDs (1994-2006) are adjusted to P by parametric (utility) or non-parametric (statistical calibration) methods. Non-parametric adjustments perform significantly better than parametric ones. Relevant as a methodological analogue, not as S&P evidence. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v31y2011i8p727-754.html)
- [V] Crisóstomo & Couso (2018), "Financial density forecasts: A comprehensive comparison of risk-neutral and historical schemes," *Journal of Forecasting* 37(5), 589-603 (also arXiv 1801.08007, CNMV WP 67). Compares 15 schemes over 21+ years. Risk-neutral densities outperform historical predictions on information content (their Integrated Forecast Score). Variance Gamma RND gives the highest out-of-sample likelihood and lowest errors. GJR-FHS (historical) is the most consistent across the density range. Lognormal, Heston and raw Breeden-Litzenberger RNDs are biased and rejected. The underlying index appears to be IBEX 35 (CNMV is the Spanish regulator); this was not confirmed from the full text. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.08007); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jforec:v:37:y:2018:i:5:p:589-603)
- [U] Aït-Sahalia & Lo (2000), "Nonparametric risk management and implied risk aversion," *Journal of Econometrics* 94, 9-51. Kernel-estimated SPD versus a historical P density for the S&P 500 gives an implied risk-aversion function (and "economic VaR"). It is non-constant and U-shaped in places. (Not re-verified this session.)
- [U] Jackwerth (2000), "Recovering risk aversion from option prices and realized returns," *Review of Financial Studies* 13(2), 433-451. Implied risk aversion for the S&P 500 becomes negative or non-monotone over some wealth ranges after the 1987 crash. This is the origin of the "pricing kernel puzzle." (Not re-verified.)
- [U] Rosenberg & Engle (2002), "Empirical pricing kernels," *Journal of Financial Economics* 64(3), 341-372. P density from an asymmetric GARCH on the S&P 500 combined with option-implied Q gives a time-varying pricing kernel. The flexible kernel is non-monotone, and risk aversion is countercyclical. (Not re-verified.)
- [V] Barone-Adesi, Engle & Mancini (2008), "A GARCH option pricing model with filtered historical simulation," *Review of Financial Studies* 21, 1223-1258. They use GARCH-FHS for P and a separately calibrated Q-GARCH. With S&P 500 data 2002-2004, the derived pricing kernel appears monotonically decreasing (secondary description). — [Beare-Schmidt WP (Princeton) cites it](https://www.princeton.edu/~erp/erp%20seminar%20pdfs/papersFall2011/Bearepkpuzzle.pdf); [Konstanz repository](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/144d454e-af08-4d56-8c32-79e2058388ac/content)
- [V] Beare & Schmidt (2016), "An empirical test of pricing kernel monotonicity," *Journal of Applied Econometrics* 31(2), 338-356, DOI 10.1002/jae.2422. A formal test on 17 years of S&P 500 options finds statistically significant monotonicity violations in a substantial proportion of months. — [Princeton WP](https://www.princeton.edu/~erp/erp%20seminar%20pdfs/papersFall2011/Bearepkpuzzle.pdf); [zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jae.2422)
- [V] Linn, Shive & Shumway (2018), "Pricing Kernel Monotonicity and Conditional Information," *Review of Financial Studies* 31(2), 493-531, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhx095. They argue standard nonparametric kernel estimators are inconsistent because they ignore conditioning information. Their conditional estimator wins in simulations. On S&P 500 (1996-2014) and FTSE 100 (2002-2014) data, the estimated kernel is consistent with monotonicity, so the puzzle is attributed to estimation flaws. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:31:y:2018:i:2:p:493-531.); [CKGSB WP PDF](https://www.ckgsb.edu.cn/uploads/201409/paper%20of%20Tyler%20Shumway.pdf)
- [V] Dalderop & Linton (Cambridge WP, CWPE2411) estimate a multiplicative pricing kernel linking risk-neutral and true conditional densities for S&P 500 options. This is a semi/nonparametric density-ratio approach. — [Janeway Institute page](https://www.janeway.econ.cam.ac.uk/node/732)

### Inferences
- Two families of Q-to-P maps exist. (a) Economic/utility maps (power or exponential utility, f_P ∝ f_Q / m) have 1-2 parameters and give interpretable risk aversion. (b) Statistical calibration maps (P-CDF = C(Q-CDF), e.g. a beta-CDF calibration as in Liu et al. 2007, Shackleton et al. 2010, and the non-parametric versions in Høg-Tsiaras) are more flexible. The calibration maps tend to win on likelihood, which suggests the "utility" interpretation matters less than correcting systematic RND miscalibration (variance risk premium, skew premium).
- The horizon split in Shackleton-Taylor-Yu is the most important result for a one-day log-score exercise. Prior S&P evidence says a pure transformed RND loses to a good historical (GARCH/HAR-type) density at 1 day, and gains appear only through blending. Short-maturity options (weeklies, 0DTE) did not exist in liquid form in their 1991-2004 sample. That is an obvious reason the 1-day result may not hold today (my inference, not a cited finding).
- The risk-aversion estimates' dependence on horizon and volatility (Bliss-Panigirtzoglou) implies that a single static calibration parameter will be misspecified. Prequential or rolling recalibration is the natural design.

### Gaps
- No paper found in this session evaluates transformed SPX RNDs at a 1-day horizon using modern short-dated (weekly/daily-expiry) options against HAR-RV or realized-GARCH densities by log score. Fan, Sandri & Taylor extend Shackleton et al. to HAR-RV with high-frequency data (seen only as a mention in search results; title, venue and findings not verified).
- The effect sizes (log-likelihood differences, test statistics) in Shackleton-Taylor-Yu and Liu et al. were not extracted, because only abstracts were read.
- Whether the Crisóstomo-Couso sample is IBEX 35 or another index needs confirmation from the full text.

## Q2. Ross (2015) recovery theorem, critiques and tests: do recovered P densities forecast returns?

### Takeaway
Theory (Borovička-Hansen-Scheinkman 2016) shows that Ross recovery identifies the true P only when the martingale component of the SDF is trivial, which is generally implausible. The most direct S&P 500 test (Jackwerth-Menner 2020) strongly rejects recovered densities against realised returns and variances, while power-utility and historical benchmarks are not rejected. Positive evidence is limited to moment-based trading (Audrino-Huitema-Ludwig 2021) and generalized recovery (Jensen-Lando-Pedersen 2019). This literature is contested and leans negative for density forecasting.

### Cited Findings
- [U] Ross (2015), "The Recovery Theorem," *Journal of Finance* 70(1), 615-648. Under a time-homogeneous Markov state and a transition-independent pricing kernel, the Perron-Frobenius eigenvector of the state-price transition matrix recovers the physical transition probabilities and the kernel. (Bibliographic details not re-verified, but the paper is universally cited.)
- [V] Borovička, Hansen & Scheinkman (2016), "Misspecified Recovery," *Journal of Finance* 71(6), 2493-2544, DOI 10.1111/jofi.12404. Perron-Frobenius extracts a positive martingale component of the SDF. The recovered measure absorbs long-term risk adjustments. If the martingale is non-trivial (as in most macro-finance models), treating the recovered measure as beliefs "distorts inference about risk-return tradeoffs." — [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/misspecified-recovery-borovi%C4%8Dka-jaroslav/10013018669); [arXiv related (Qin-Linetsky 1411.3075)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.3075)
- [V] Jackwerth & Menner (2020), "Does the Ross recovery theorem work empirically?" *Journal of Financial Economics* 137(3), 723-739, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.03.006. Recovered S&P 500 physical distributions are incompatible with future returns and realised variances, and the rejection gets stronger when economically reasonable constraints are added. Simple benchmarks (power-utility-transformed RND, historical return distribution) cannot be rejected. Tests used: Berkowitz, the Knüppel (2015) uniformity test, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov. Causes: the strong assumptions, difficulty estimating transition state prices, and poor simultaneous fit to short- and long-dated options. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:137:y:2020:i:3:p:723-739); [KOPS Konstanz](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/abb73d8c-ce5d-4111-9326-fa379c96f43c/full); [AEA 2018 WP](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2018/preliminary/paper/yNn4GdY9)
- [V] Audrino, Huitema & Ludwig (2021), "An Empirical Implementation of the Ross Recovery Theorem as a Prediction Device," *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 19(2), 291-312. A nonparametric recovery from SPD surfaces (Ludwig's method), applied to S&P 500 options, 2000-2012. Market-timing strategies using recovered moments outperform those using risk-neutral moments. Only the abstract was verified, and no density log-score evaluation is reported there. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v19y2021i2p291-312..html); [Alexandria St. Gallen](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/256491)
- [V] Jensen, Lando & Pedersen (2019), "Generalized Recovery," *Journal of Financial Economics* 133(1), 154-174, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.12.003. Drops Ross's stationarity/time-homogeneity. Recovery is possible when the number of observed maturities exceeds the number of states (or kernel parameters). They test the predictive power of the recovered expected return and other statistics. The specific coefficients and R² were not retrieved in this session. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:133:y:2019:i:1:p:154-174); [CEPR DP12665](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:12665)

### Inferences
- For one-day P density forecasting, recovery methods are a weak candidate. The strongest out-of-sample S&P test rejects them, and the benchmarks a project would compare against (power-utility RND, historical) survive the same tests.
- Positive results (Audrino et al.) are about economic value of recovered moments for timing, which is a different and weaker target than calibrated full densities.

### Gaps
- The JLP (2019) empirical magnitudes (e.g. sign and significance of recovered-expected-return predictive regressions) were not verified.
- No study found that scores Ross or generalized-recovery densities by log score at a daily horizon.

## Q3. Martin (2017) SVIX bound, Martin & Wagner (2019), Chabi-Yo & Loudis (2020), and use for forecasting P moments

### Takeaway
Option prices give model-light lower (and, for Chabi-Yo-Loudis, upper) bounds on the conditional equity premium. Martin's SVIX bound averages about 5% a year and spikes above 20% in 2008. Chabi-Yo-Loudis bounds average about 5.2%. These are mean forecasts, useful mainly at monthly-to-annual horizons. Using them as constraints improves out-of-sample equity-premium forecasts. They imply that the P mean moves strongly with Q-variance. At a 1-day horizon the mean is a second-order part of the log score.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Martin (2017), "What is the expected return on the market?" *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 132(1), 367-433. Under a negative-correlation condition, the equity premium is bounded below by risk-neutral variance (SVIX²). The bound is very volatile, exceeding 20% at the 2008 peak. Its time-series average is about 5%, suggesting it is approximately tight. High premia in stress periods reflect high short-run expected returns. — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/67036); [FMG DP750](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP750.pdf)
- [U] Martin & Wagner (2019), "What is the expected return on a stock?" *Journal of Finance* 74(4), 1887-1929. Extends the approach to individual stocks using stock-level and index SVIX. Not re-verified this session.
- [V] Chabi-Yo & Loudis (2020), "The conditional expected market return," *Journal of Financial Economics* 137(3), 752-786, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.03.009. Lower and upper bounds on the conditional expected excess market return are tied to risk-neutral volatility, skewness and kurtosis. They need no-arbitrage only (no distributional assumptions or history) and are computable in real time. The bounds are highly volatile, positively skewed and fat-tailed. The average expected excess return is about 5.2%. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:137:y:2020:i:3:p:752-786)
- [V] Constrained equity-premium forecasting that uses the Martin (2017) and Chabi-Yo-Loudis (2020) lower bounds as constraints improves out-of-sample forecasts (Wang & Zhou, per the search summary; the venue appears to be *Management Science* 70(10), 2024, 6804-6834, from the IDEAS link, but the author and paper match was not confirmed). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v70y2024i10p6804-6834.html)
- [V] A 2026 arXiv paper estimates the SDF from option prices to predict the equity premium. It describes Chabi-Yo-Loudis as extending Martin with risk-neutral moments up to order four. — [arXiv 2607.08500](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08500)

### Inferences
- These bounds give a theory-grounded way to set the P mean (drift) of a transformed density. For example, shift Q by an amount ≥ SVIX²·Δt instead of fitting a free risk-aversion parameter. The mean matters little at 1 day (daily premium is a few basis points against ~1% daily volatility), so gains in daily log score must come from scale and shape, not the mean.

### Gaps
- No work found that turns SVIX or Chabi-Yo-Loudis bounds into full P density forecasts evaluated by log score.

## Q4. Option-implied moments (VIX, BKM skew/kurtosis, VRP) as predictors of realized volatility, tails and returns; implied versus historical volatility forecasting

### Takeaway
Implied volatility is among the best-established findings. It carries incremental, often dominant, information for future realized volatility compared with historical and high-frequency measures (Blair-Poon-Taylor 2001: VIX best out of sample at 1-20 days). Implied volatility stays informative after controlling for continuous and jump RV components (Busch-Christensen-Nielsen 2011). It is biased upward because of the variance risk premium, which itself predicts returns (Bollerslev-Tauchen-Zhou 2009). For density forecasting, the volatility (scale) channel is where Q information most reliably helps, provided the VRP bias is corrected.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Blair, Poon & Taylor (2001), *Journal of Econometrics* 105, 5-26. S&P 100 index volatility at 1-20 day horizons. In sample, nearly all relevant information is in VIX, with little incremental information in high-frequency returns. Out of sample, VIX gives the most accurate forecasts for all horizons and measures considered. — [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/10896152.pdf)
- [V] Busch, Christensen & Nielsen (2011), *Journal of Econometrics* 160(1), 48-57. A HAR model with RV split into continuous and jump parts, plus implied volatility, across FX, stock and bond markets. IV has incremental information beyond both components and is unbiased for future RV in FX and stock markets (working-paper wording). Jump components are partly predictable, and options appear to incorporate information about future jumps. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:econom:v:160:y:2011:i:1:p:48-57); [CREATES RP 07-09](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/07/rp07_09.pdf)
- [U] Christensen & Prabhala (1998), "The relation between implied and realized volatility," *Journal of Financial Economics* 50(2), 125-150. S&P 100, monthly non-overlapping data. IV outperforms past RV and subsumes it in some specifications. Earlier negative results (e.g. Canina-Figlewski 1993) are attributed to overlapping data and the 1987 regime shift. Not re-verified.
- [U] Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009), "Expected stock returns and variance risk premia," *Review of Financial Studies* 22(11), 4463-4492. VRP = VIX² − expected/realized variance. It predicts quarterly S&P 500 excess returns better than P/E, P/D and similar predictors, with peak R² around the quarterly horizon. Its predictive power at daily/monthly horizons is weaker. Not re-verified.
- [U] Bakshi, Kapadia & Madan (2003), "Stock return characteristics, skew laws, and the differential pricing of individual equity options," *Review of Financial Studies* 16(1), 101-143. Model-free risk-neutral variance, skewness and kurtosis from OTM option portfolios (the "BKM moments"). Not re-verified. Evidence on BKM skewness predicting physical skewness or tails at short horizons is mixed, and no specific study was verified this session.

### Inferences
- A practical Q-to-P density design for 1 day takes scale from a VRP-corrected implied variance (or a HAR-RV + IV regression à la Busch et al.) and possibly shape from RND skewness, scaled down. This matches the literature's pattern that IV helps volatility while the full RND shape, untransformed, is miscalibrated.
- The VRP means raw Q variance overstates P variance on average. A daily density built directly from Q will be too wide, so log score is penalised unless recalibrated. This is consistent with the RND-loses-at-1-day result of Shackleton et al. (2010).

### Gaps
- No verified study in this session documents whether BKM skew/kurtosis improve daily P density log scores beyond IV.
- The magnitude of IV's incremental R² in daily RV forecasting with modern data (post-2010, with weekly options and VIX9D/VIX1D) was not verified.

## Q5. Density forecast combination of option-implied and historical densities; ML/neural approaches (2018-2026)

### Takeaway
Blending is the most consistently supported outcome. Liu et al. (2007) and Shackleton et al. (2010) both find that mixtures of transformed-RND and historical densities beat each component, especially at short horizons. Non-parametric statistical calibration beats parametric utility transforms (Høg-Tsiaras 2011). Neural work since 2016 is mostly about estimating arbitrage-free RNDs or pricing options. No verified peer-reviewed study was found that uses ML to map Q to P and evaluates daily S&P 500 P-density log scores against strong time-series benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Liu, Shackleton, Taylor & Xu (2007): a combination of transformed real-world and historical densities is the preferred predictive density. — [Lancaster eprints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/44582/)
- [V] Shackleton, Taylor & Yu (2010): mixtures of real-world and historical densities have higher likelihoods than either component at short horizons (S&P 500, 1991-2004). — [Lancaster PDF](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/45542/1/10.pdf)
- [V] Høg & Tsiaras (2011): non-parametric (calibration) Q-to-P adjustments significantly beat parametric adjustments for crude oil. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v31y2011i8p727-754.html)
- [V] Ivanova & Puigvert (2014), "Interest rate forecasts, state price densities and risk premium from Euribor options," *Journal of Banking & Finance* 48, 210-223. Interest-rate options, not equity. Only the citation was seen. Findings not verified. — [found as a reference in search; IFDP list](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econresdata/ifdp/2014/files/ifdp1122.pdf)
- [V] A related working paper, "Option-Implied Preferences Adjustments and Risk-Neutral Density Forecasts" (UPV/EHU WP), surfaced but was not read. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/p/ehu/dfaeii/6740.html)
- [U] Kitsul & Wright (2013), "The economics of options-implied inflation probability density functions," *Journal of Financial Economics* 110(3), 696-711. Inflation caps/floors RNDs, with an empirical pricing kernel from comparing RND quantiles to realised inflation. Not re-verified.
- [V] Neural RND/pricing work: Gated neural networks with no-arbitrage constraints yield valid RNDs on S&P 500 options (arXiv 1609.07472). Risk-Neutral Generative Networks (Xian et al., arXiv 2405.17770, 2024) cover S&P 500 options 1996-2023. A Deep Log-Sum-Exp network with transfer learning for sparse/illiquid strikes, tested on SPX (Conti & Morelli, arXiv 2512.11731, 2025). A NTU thesis uses deep nets to map historical P densities plus macro variables to future RNDs (the reverse direction, unreviewed). — [arXiv 1609.07472](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07472); [arXiv 2405.17770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17770v1); [papers.cool 2512.11731](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2512.11731); [NTU thesis](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/93087?locale=en)
- [V] Dalderop & Linton density-ratio pricing kernel estimation for the S&P 500 (non-neural, semiparametric) is the closest modern econometric Q-to-P link found. — [Janeway](https://www.janeway.econ.cam.ac.uk/node/732)

### Inferences
- The robust design from this literature: (i) a flexible statistical calibration of Q to P instead of a utility kernel, (ii) estimated prequentially, (iii) blended with a strong historical density, with weights chosen by out-of-sample log score. Any claimed 1-day gain should be measured relative to the blend, not only to the historical model.
- Neural SDF work in asset pricing (e.g. Chen-Pelger-Zhu, "Deep learning in asset pricing," Management Science 2024 [U]) concerns cross-sectional SDFs from characteristics, not option-implied P densities. It is a different problem.

### Gaps
- No verified ML paper (2018-2026) found that learns a Q-to-P map for the S&P 500 and reports daily log-score gains over GARCH/HAR densities. This looks like a genuine gap, though a narrower search could find working papers.
- Optimal combination weights (static versus time-varying) for RND/historical blends at a 1-day horizon are not reported in the abstracts seen.

## Q6. Summary: KNOWN versus CONTESTED versus MISSING (focus: one-day-ahead P density log score)

### Takeaway
KNOWN: implied volatility is highly informative for future volatility, RNDs must be transformed to be used as P forecasts, transformed RNDs beat historical densities at 2-4 week horizons, historical densities beat transformed RNDs at 1 day, and blends beat both. CONTESTED: the shape of the pricing kernel and whether Ross-type recovery works. MISSING: modern-data, short-dated-option, 1-day log-score evaluations of transformed or blended RNDs against strong realized-volatility benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- KNOWN, horizon split: 2-4 week, transformed RND > historical. 1 day, historical > transformed RND. Short horizons, mixture > both (S&P 500, 1991-2004, out-of-sample log-likelihood). — [Shackleton, Taylor & Yu 2010](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/45542/1/10.pdf)
- KNOWN, blending helps: combination of transformed and historical densities is preferred. — [Liu et al. 2007](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/44582/)
- KNOWN, risk aversion implied by calibrating RNDs falls with horizon and is lower in high volatility, so calibration is not stable. — [Bliss & Panigirtzoglou 2004](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/option-implied-risk-aversion-estimates-bliss-robert/10005303188)
- KNOWN, IV dominates or adds information for volatility forecasting at 1-20 days. — [Blair, Poon & Taylor 2001](https://core.ac.uk/download/10896152.pdf); [Busch, Christensen & Nielsen 2011](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:econom:v:160:y:2011:i:1:p:48-57)
- KNOWN (economic value, monthly): risk-adjusted implied distributions beat historical ones for S&P 500 market timing, net of costs, though weaker in the subprime crisis. — [Kostakis et al. 2011](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:57:y:2011:i:7:p:1231-1249)
- CONTESTED, pricing kernel puzzle: violations of monotonicity are significant in many months (Beare & Schmidt 2016) versus "an artefact of estimators ignoring conditioning information" (Linn, Shive & Shumway 2018) and a monotone GARCH-FHS kernel (Barone-Adesi, Engle & Mancini 2008). — [Beare-Schmidt](https://www.princeton.edu/~erp/erp%20seminar%20pdfs/papersFall2011/Bearepkpuzzle.pdf); [LSS](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:31:y:2018:i:2:p:493-531.)
- CONTESTED, recovery: theoretically misspecified (BHS 2016) and empirically rejected for S&P densities (Jackwerth-Menner 2020), versus positive trading value of recovered moments (Audrino et al. 2021) and generalized recovery (JLP 2019). — [Jackwerth-Menner](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:137:y:2020:i:3:p:723-739); [Audrino et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v19y2021i2p291-312..html)
- CONTESTED, whether RNDs beat historical densities overall depends on the scoring rule and model. Crisóstomo-Couso find RNDs (Variance Gamma) win on likelihood and information content, while GJR-FHS is most consistent across the density range. Lognormal, Heston and raw Breeden-Litzenberger RNDs are rejected. — [Crisóstomo & Couso 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.08007)

### Inferences
- A project finding that a recalibrated or blended option-implied density beats a historical density at 1 day would go beyond the main S&P evidence (Shackleton et al. 2010). It should therefore be framed as conditional on (a) modern short-dated options, (b) prequential recalibration, and (c) blending. A failure to beat historical at 1 day would replicate the literature, not contradict it.
- Because the Bliss-Panigirtzoglou risk aversion is horizon- and regime-dependent, and the VRP biases Q variance upward, "width" (scale) calibration probably drives most of any 1-day log-score difference. Width-controlled comparisons are the right diagnostic.
